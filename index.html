Professional Self-Assessment

Completing the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University has given me the opportunity to develop a broad range of technical and problem-solving skills while also helping me clarify the type of work I want to pursue after graduation. When I began the program, my primary career goal was to become a software engineer. As I progressed through the coursework and gained more practical experience, my interests evolved. I am now particularly interested in combining software development with my professional experience in hardware testing and calibration. I am interested in hardware test automation because it would allow me to use programming to automate testing and calibration processes, collect and analyze data, and improve the efficiency and reliability of hardware systems. The skills I developed throughout the Computer Science program have given me a stronger foundation for pursuing this direction.

My coursework has strengthened my ability to approach problems from both a technical and practical perspective. In software engineering and full-stack development, I learned how different components of an application work together and how design decisions affect maintainability and usability. My work with the Travlr Getaways application gave me experience with Angular, Node.js, Express, MongoDB, Mongoose, REST APIs, and software architecture. I also gained experience with Python and MongoDB through database and CRUD-related coursework, where I learned how to store, retrieve, manipulate, and present data. In other courses, I worked with artificial intelligence and machine learning concepts, including neural networks and reinforcement learning. My work with Android development and OpenGL graphics also expanded my understanding of application interfaces, user interaction, graphics programming, and the relationship between software and the underlying computing environment. These experiences have helped me become more comfortable learning new technologies and applying computer science concepts to different types of problems.

The program also strengthened my understanding of data structures and algorithms. Through coursework and the enhancement of my Travlr application, I learned that an application needs more than functional code; it needs efficient ways to process and organize information. I applied filtering, sorting, price processing, and pagination to trip data and considered how these operations affect the efficiency of a system. This experience helped me better understand how algorithmic decisions can affect performance as the amount of data increases.

My understanding of software engineering and databases has also developed significantly. I learned to organize applications into appropriate components, separate responsibilities, work with APIs, and use version control and structured development practices. I also learned that databases should not simply store information but should help protect its integrity. In the Travlr project, I worked with MongoDB and Mongoose to define data structures, validate information, enforce unique values, and use indexes to improve data access. These experiences taught me to consider reliability and maintainability when designing software rather than focusing only on whether an application works.

Security has become another important part of how I evaluate software. Throughout the program, I learned that security needs to be considered during design and development rather than added after an application is completed. Validation, authentication, controlled access to data, and careful handling of user input can reduce vulnerabilities and prevent invalid or unexpected information from affecting an application. Working with authentication and REST APIs in the Travlr application helped me recognize the importance of protecting both application functionality and stored data.

Communication and collaboration have also been important parts of my development. Although much of my coursework involved individual projects, I learned to develop software in ways that make the work understandable and maintainable by other developers. Using Git branches, organizing code into logical components, documenting changes, participating in code reviews, and responding to instructor feedback helped me develop practices that are important in collaborative development environments. I also learned to think about different stakeholders and audiences when developing software. A developer may focus on implementation details, while a user or stakeholder is more concerned with whether the system is reliable, understandable, efficient, and capable of solving the intended problem. Learning to communicate technical decisions clearly has helped me become better prepared to work with both technical and nontechnical audiences.

Developing my CS 499 ePortfolio has helped me recognize the strengths I have developed throughout the program. Rather than simply listing courses or technologies, the portfolio provides evidence of my ability to take existing software, evaluate it, identify areas for improvement, and implement meaningful changes. It has also helped me identify areas where I want to continue growing. I plan to continue developing my programming, automation, and software engineering skills through professional experience and certifications rather than immediately pursuing a graduate degree. My goal is to apply these skills to hardware testing and calibration automation, where software can improve testing efficiency, consistency, and data collection.

The artifacts included in this ePortfolio demonstrate different areas of my computer science knowledge while working together as parts of the Travlr Getaways application. The software design and engineering artifact, trip-data.service.ts, demonstrates how a frontend application communicates with a backend REST API and includes improvements to organization and error handling. The algorithms and data structures artifact, trips.js, demonstrates the use of filtering, sorting, price processing, and pagination to process trip data efficiently. The database artifact, travlr.js, demonstrates how database schemas can enforce data integrity, validation, uniqueness, performance, and security. Together, these artifacts show how the different layers of a full-stack application interact and how decisions made in one layer can affect the others

The ePortfolio demonstrates my progression from learning individual computer science concepts to applying them to a functioning software system. The artifacts provide evidence of my skills in software engineering, algorithms and data structures, databases, security, problem solving, testing, and technical communication. They also demonstrate my ability to evaluate existing solutions, identify areas for improvement, implement and test changes, and explain my technical decisions. These are skills I plan to continue developing as I pursue opportunities in hardware testing and calibration automation.
